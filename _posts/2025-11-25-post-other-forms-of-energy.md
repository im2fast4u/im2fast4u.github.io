## Einstein's Equation of Energy
Back in the 20th century, Albert Einstein developed a famous formula for calculating energy. The formula is $E = mc^2$, where $E$ is the energy in joules, $m$ is the mass, and $c$ is the speed of light. This means that the more mass an object has, the more energy it has. An object with a mass of 35 kilograms will have an energy of approximately $3.15 \times 10^{18}$ joules, while an object with a mass of 30 kilograms will have an energy of approximately $2.7 \times 10^{18}$ joules. As you can see, the 35 kilogram object has a greater energy. However, there is another way to prove this statement. The speed of light is a positive constant, so the bigger number you are multiply it to, the bigger result you will get. For example, if you multiply every number by 5, the bigger the number you are multiplying 5 with, the bigger answer you will get.
