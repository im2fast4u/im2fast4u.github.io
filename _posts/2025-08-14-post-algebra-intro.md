Algebra can sometimes be hard. But you're in luck because I am going to tell you how you can ace algebra! I will first give you a quick intro and tell you what algebra is about, and then I will show you the basics of algebra: how functions work, and how to solve for a variable. After, I will show you the more advanced parts of algebra such as linear algebra. So now let's get into all of it!

## What Is Algebra About?
Algebra is a not-very-easy subject for math that helps us develop and create new inventions. In algebra, letters, also known as variables, are replaced with numbers in a mathematical sentence. That is because we don't know what that number is. It is an unknown number. You solve for those variables by figuring out what number is being replaced by them. That is what the next section is about.

## Solving For X
If you are solving for a variable, the variable is most likely going to be x. X is the most common variable when it comes to algebra. Suppose we have the linear equation 4x - 5 = 19 (linear equations are in the form of y = mx + b. This is explained in more detail in the next section). We need to make the equation become x = value of x. We can do that by multiply/divide and add/subtract numbers on both sides of the equation. If we add 5 to both sides of the equation we get the new equation 4x = 24. Now, all we need to do is divide both sides by 4, and we get x = 6. We found the value of the variable!

## A Simple Function
In the last section I told you about linear equations. They come in the form y = mx + b, or f(x) = mx + b. The 'm' variable represents the slope[^1] of the equation, and the 'b' variable represents the y-coordinate of the y-intercept[^2] of the equation. The function I am going to use is y = 3x - 4. Whatever number/input we give the function, we get an output. Suppose our input is 3. If the 'x' variable represents the input, and the 'y' variable represents the output, our output would be 3(3) - 4, and acording to PEMDAS, that is 5. 

## Linear Algebra
Linear algebra studies vectors, and matrices. It is an advanced subject part of algebra. I am going to tell you what vectors are about in this part of the blog post. So, vectors are made up of two parts. They are the direction, and the magnitude. The direction represents where the vectors are moving. The magnitude is the length of the vector. For example, velocity can be a vector. I am not going to go too deep into this, but let's say the velocity at which I am running at is 5 mph east. The direction is east, and the magnitude (or length) is 5 mph.
